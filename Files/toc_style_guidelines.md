Style Guidelines for TOC Chapters Generation

1. Voice & Tone
   - Friendly, neutral, and informative.
   - No motivational, emotional, or promotional language.
   - Use active voice.

2. Readability
   - Keep sentences short: 8–16 words.
   - Avoid complex grammar or nested clauses.
   - Prefer clarity over technical depth at TOC stage.

3. Chapter Titles
   - Must be clear, specific, and short.
   - Each title focuses on ONE concept only.
   - Avoid vague titles like “Advanced Concepts” unless the course requires it.

4. Short Descriptions
   - Exactly 1–2 sentences.
   - Provide a quick idea of what the learner will understand by that chapter.
   - Avoid long lists or examples — keep it high-level.
   - Should sound like a preview, not a full lesson.

5. Ordering
   - Chapters must progress logically (beginner → intermediate → advanced) unless course title demands it (e.g., "Advanced Python" should start with advanced topics).
   - First 3–5 chapters should introduce fundamentals.
   - Final chapters can include summary, project, or next steps.

6. Vocabulary
   - Use simple, international English.
   - Avoid idioms or region-specific references.
   - Avoid slang.

7. No Redundancy
   - Do not reuse descriptions or phrases across chapters.
   - Each chapter must be uniquely meaningful.

8. Consistent Formatting
   - Title Case for chapter titles.
   - Sentences must end with periods.
   - No emojis or symbols.

9. Generalization
   - Since TOC is high-level, avoid heavy math or code unless chapter seed demands it.
   - Keep descriptions conceptual, not procedural.

10. Mobile-first Writing
   - Short, scannable sentences.
   - Avoid comma-heavy or clause-heavy constructions.
